# Physiological trajectories surrounding red blood cell transfusion in intensive care patients and their associations with blood unit characteristics

The authors studied seven cardiovascular and respiratory measures in adult intensive care units in Stockholm. The dashboard shows how these measures changed in the 12 hours before and after transfusion start and compares the two-hour change across blood unit characteristics. [Open the dashboard](https://scandat.github.io/Publication-Dashboard/)

## Study methods and results

The [analysis guide](analysis/README.md) contains annotated excerpts from the programs used to select the cohorts, fit the models and calculate the reported comparisons. These excerpts are for inspecting the calculations and do not constitute a standalone analysis program. The [input dictionary](analysis/INPUTS.md) describes the study extracts and the transfusion-record coverage used for the follow-up restriction. The [data index](aggregate-data/reader.json) lists the 317 aggregate result files displayed by the dashboard and identifies each by its SHA-256 checksum.

| Under `aggregate-data/results/` | How to read the file |
| --- | --- |
| `main/` | One modelled trajectory for each cohort, model and outcome. Each minute from −720 to +720 has an estimate and its lower and upper pointwise 95% confidence limits. `levels` gives predicted values and `changes` gives differences from minute −60. |
| `endpoints/` | Predicted change from minute −60 to +60, followed by its lower and upper confidence limits. |
| `components/` | `within` gives the two-hour change in each reported category. `pairs` compares each category with each later one in the [reported category order](src/data/componentDisplay.ts), including only Monday to Friday for donation weekday. Each row gives the first category’s change minus the second’s, its confidence limits, the unadjusted p value and the within-family Holm-adjusted p value. `overall` gives the tests for the two-hour change and trajectory shape. A status of `COMPUTED` means the test returned a result. |
| `observed/` | Smoothed primary-cohort measurements on the recorded (`native`) and standardized scales. The 81 curves correspond to smoothing spans of 10% through 90%. Each curve contains a fit status, 1,441 estimates ordered by minute and a list of gaps. `DIRECT_COMPLETED` denotes a completed fit. Each gap gives its first index, the first index after it and the reason for the missing estimates. Index zero is minute −720. `OUTSIDE_RETAINED_SUPPORT` marks times outside the fitted range. Curve values are rounded to two decimal places on the recorded scale and three on the standardized scale. |
| `tables/` | Mean and standard deviation of event averages in the before and after windows, using events with readings in both. The files also give the mean paired change with its confidence limits, and counts for each outcome. The `modelPatients`, `modelEvents` and `modelMeasurements` counts apply to both BASE and FULL, which had identical counts for that outcome and cohort. |
| `study/` | Patient, admission, event and blood unit characteristics for each cohort. For SAPS 3, `invalid` counts nonnumeric entries. `missing` counts missing scores. `notRequested` counts patients when the analysis omitted the SAPS 3 summary. An empty array for `missing` or `notRequested` omits the corresponding row. `allPopulationsComplete` indicates whether all three cohorts had a SAPS 3 entry for every patient, including nonnumeric entries. For blood unit characteristics, `coverage` holds a note on missing or excluded units when applicable. |
| `figures/` | Counts for the cohort flow diagram. Each pair in `selection` gives the number of patients entering a step and the number excluded at that step. `primary` gives the numbers of patients, admissions, events and RBC units. The two `sensitivity` entries give patient and event counts for the first-event and no-further-transfusion cohorts, respectively. |

Where present, `schema` or `schemaVersion` identifies the file format, `releaseId` identifies the data release and `key` identifies the result within the index.

On the recorded scale, changes in inspired oxygen fraction and peripheral oxygen saturation are expressed in percentage points. Pairwise p values are shown to three decimal places or as <0.001. The pressure codes `ARTm`, `ARTs` and `ARTd` denote mean, systolic and diastolic arterial pressure. `HR` is heart rate, `FIO2` inspired oxygen fraction, `SPO2` peripheral oxygen saturation and `VE` minute ventilation. `PRIMARY`, `FIRST_EVENT` and `NO_FOLLOWING_UNIT_12H` identify the three cohorts. `BASE` and `FULL` identify the two adjustment models.

In the descriptive cohort tables, donor sex combines male and unclassified units, parity percentages use female-donor units as the denominator, and weekend donations are grouped together. Units per patient are shown individually through four, then in bands of 5–9 and 10 or more. Table 2 shows observed means and standard deviations to one decimal place and paired changes and their limits to two.

For example, Table 2 reports a fully adjusted increase in mean arterial pressure of 1.41 mmHg (95% CI 1.26 to 1.56). Those three values come from `panels.ARTm.PRIMARY` in the [FULL endpoints file](aggregate-data/results/endpoints/FULL.json). The observed change of 2.08 mmHg (1.73 to 2.42) comes from `rows.ARTm.change` in the [primary cohort table](aggregate-data/results/tables/primary.json). That comparison pairs readings from the same event. The smoothed curves give equal weight to selected measurement times, with different events contributing at different times, so the two summaries can move in different directions.

## Run the dashboard

With Node.js 24 and npm, run `npm ci` and `npm run dev` from the repository root and open the local address printed by Vite. Use `npm run build` for a static build and `npm run preview` to serve it locally.

The Swedish Ethical Review Authority approved the study (2021-04890). Access to individual clinical records requires ethical approval and permission from the relevant data holders.
